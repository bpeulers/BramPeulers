Smart navigation for higher conversion on your website

Your website is essentially your company’s digital business card. It’s where you present your services and products and attract new customers. A good navigation structure is therefore very important – for your visibility in Google, but also to help visitors find relevant information as quickly as possible. In short: a solid foundation gives your conversion a positive boost.

Where do you start?

It’s tempting to dive straight into your website’s content. Still, it’s wise to think about the structure first. The time you invest in the navigation structure can save you hours of work later. A good plan is half the battle.

Start with a good tree structure

Your website’s tree structure shows its hierarchy at a glance. You can use this structure for the navigation menu, but also in the footer. The benefit of a clear structure is that Google reads it the same way your visitors do, which has a positive effect on your search engine visibility (SEO). Visitors can also find what they’re looking for quickly, which leads to good conversion (CTR). It works the other way round too: if the structure falls short, your search ranking suffers and your bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave your site immediately) goes up.

Define your main navigation

So how do you organise the main menu? Start by putting the homepage at the top of the tree – it’s the most important page of your website. Next to the homepage you add a number of other main themes (pillar pages). Say you run a bakery: you could organise your website as Bread, Pastry, Holidays, Team, Blog, Contact. The next step is to define sub-themes (the landing pages) that belong to the main themes. For the bakery, these could be pages such as St Nicholas, King’s Day, Christmas, New Year’s Eve and Easter under ‘Holidays’.

Think from the visitor’s perspective

What works well in this example is that the website is organised around the visitor rather than the company’s own view. So always try to base it on data or user research. With Google Analytics you can analyse how users navigate your website, and with user research you can find out what visitors would look for on your site. Base your main menu on these findings. From this data you could also create personas of your target audience.

Guide your visitor

Now that the tree structure is clear, you can organise the navigation menu. Keep your visitors’ customer journeys in mind: how does your visitor move through the website, and what end goal do they want to reach? By steering on this, visitors quickly find relevant information and get a good overview of the site. A tip to help visitors find things even faster is adding a search bar and a prominent contact button to the main menu. Don’t make the main menu too extensive: use a maximum of 6 main themes and a maximum of 6 sub-themes per theme.

Organising the footer

With the main menu in place, you can move on to the footer (the bottom of your page). What’s often overlooked is that many visitors scroll down and then lose their overview of the website, because the main menu is no longer in view. It’s therefore wise to repeat the tree structure in the footer. The footer is also a good place for your contact details and links to your social media channels. There are often a few ‘other pages’ that matter to have on your website but are less relevant to visitors, such as a privacy policy or company information. Those can easily be ‘tucked away’ in the footer.
